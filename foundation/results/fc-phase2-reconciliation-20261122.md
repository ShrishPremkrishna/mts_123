## What the five reported problems actually establish

### Distant robot tag and unreliable heading

The reported 8 cm tag occupying about 26 by 10 pixels is consistent with insufficient sampling and foreshortening, but the raw frame is missing. Focus, blur, paper bending, mounting angle and decoder behavior remain possible contributors. A larger rigid, flat tag is a strong experiment, not a demonstrated cure. Main's retry targets missing floor IDs rather than specifically a missing robot ID; Tej adds a last-position crop and relaxed decoding that need timing and false-ID evaluation.

The learned heading correction uses walking displacement as truth. That mixes tag bias with gait sideways drift, actual body turning, floor effects and possible IMU problems. First measure stationary body heading independently. Wrong uniform tag height/forward offset affects position under the ideal horizontal-plane model; it does not by itself explain heading-dependent yaw error. The user's earlier successful nose-aligned tag observation remains valid for that setup; Tej's later remount is a different observation.

### Jitter after several minutes

The raw kickseen4 recording reproduces the deterioration. An early 10-second window has tilt 0.704–0.885 degrees, median gyro 0.0119 rad/s and approximately zero joint range. A late window has tilt 24.705–34.258 degrees, median gyro 0.2351 rad/s and joint range 0.676 rad, with no nonzero velocity messages in that window. External physical stillness and causal ordering are not recorded. Sensor error, actual body movement and feedback oscillation cannot yet be separated.

Late bad windows retain approximately 20.2 ms inference cadence and short IMU publication gaps. That weakens a sustained scheduling-stall explanation; it does not eliminate every timing/load effect. Session6 has much better late telemetry, but restart, temperature, calibration history and motion history differ. CPU temperature is not IMU temperature. The two preserved still-test temperature samples are insufficient for a thermal diagnosis.

The actual u6 driver is the older November 2 installation, selected by its exact binary hash. Its report-publication and batch-timing handling lack fixes already in main. Those are narrow software improvements worth retaining, but are not proof of the cause of minutes-long jitter.

The FC match driver already requests Game Rotation Vector and another gyro report from the **same BNO chip**. These are not independent sensors. Fresh but biased primary readings can remain accepted. Switching orientation report cannot be claimed to cure gyro bias, accelerometer errors or a mechanical oscillation. The manufacturer's [BNO08X datasheet, section 2.2.2](https://www.ceva-ip.com/wp-content/uploads/BNO080_085-Datasheet.pdf) explains that Game Rotation Vector excludes magnetometer corrections and allows yaw drift; this makes it a comparison candidate, not a qualified fix.

### FC startup faults

Four recovered FC logs show two joint-exchange faults and two first-claim agreement-expiry faults, each within 1.6 ms after activation success. They are distinct from late-session jitter. The generic “IMU fault” label incorrectly groups joint failures with sensor admission failures.

Primary reports were fresh, but the first-claim gate also depends on the alternate reports, accuracy, agreement history, receipt overlap and a generation/deadline. Existing logs omit the values needed to identify which predicate failed. Capture one complete, bounded fault snapshot before changing that gate. The older Home/walking bounds mismatch remains a supported mechanism; the exact tripping joint in these lines is unknown. The subsequent changed Home pose also has a recorded rear-knee return failure.

### High latency

Recovered server logs establish that the last five-job block spans 18.868 seconds: 6.196 seconds in active jobs and 12.672 seconds between them. The remainder includes measurement, settling, polling, networking and potentially operator/bridge time; it cannot be assigned entirely to one component. Two earlier blocks have 28.064 and 29.904 seconds outside active jobs. One block has 11 logged jobs where the handoff says 12; retain the discrepancy until the Mac ledger is available.

Onboard answers report 214–371 ms frame ages, median 257.5 ms across seen answers in kickseen6. One logged answer breaks per-frame work into decode 17.6, view 25.1, YOLO 127.8 and fit 26.0 ms. Those medians and frame ages overlap; do not sum them into a request latency. The complete 22–47 second cycle and distant-tag wait durations still need Mac logs.

The bridge serializes finite motion, polling, settling and multi-frame measurements. It does not continuously process overhead frames during ordinary movement. Reuse FC's existing continuous approach feedback and finite exclusive kick, with measured timing, rather than adding another motion owner. Hailo conversion is a later experiment if the CPU detector remains the measured bottleneck.

### Replacement ball detector and freezes on lost localization

An offline replay of exact FC detector code reproduces projection-dependent rejection: an ideal sphere is stretched near the panorama pole and fails pixel roundness tests. With recovered mount assumptions, pink detection fails at body x=0.27 m; at x=0.249 m even a perfect semantic box fails. This is a software-mechanism reproduction, not a physical cutoff or accuracy measurement.

Ball Eye already supplies stereographic floor projection, angular-size range and current-image outline fitting. Preserve this useful geometry. It can also return a box-only estimate, and its mount defaults are estimated. Actual configuration uses radius 0.091 m, conflicting with main's 0.11 m; physical ball size must decide. The near-track fallback requires a new outline measurement, which should be preserved. However, one FC-input path promotes a fix even when the tracker rejected it as a jump.

The bridge alternates between long waits and up to six dead-reckoned jobs, including a kick. Additional exact-source probes show: an old robot sample receives a newer ball timestamp; invalid old/future onboard timestamps become apparent age 130 ms; an old onboard correction survives failed observations; missing IMU health permits a kick; a no-kick run can change saved kick calibration; and a late HTTP result can be accepted past its deadline. These defects are independent of whether they occurred in Tej's run. Removing all holds would hide uncertainty rather than restore reliable feedback.
