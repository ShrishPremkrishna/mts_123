# Perception workstream

Own Optional Labs 1 and 3 and give Pupper the vision it needs to approach a soccer ball around an obstacle. On offense, Pupper will line up behind the ball and drive through it with its head toward the opponent’s colored goal. On defense, it will start in front of its own goal and move laterally to intercept a rolling shot. A clear floor, tape, a ball, two colored goal markers and one soft obstacle are enough for the first trials.

Work from the team repository on `main`. Use `final_project/perception/` for soccer code, `follow_me_pupper/` for reusable Lab 5 components, and use Tej’s separate branch as the reference for his Optional Lab 1 work. [Team repository](https://github.com/ShrishPremkrishna/mts_123)

## Prove the soccer fundamentals

1. **Measure what the camera sees.** Start with the installed YOLO detector’s `sports ball` class. With Pupper stationary, record the actual ball at marked distances and bearings, rolling, hidden and absent. Include both goal colors and the last few centimetres by its nose. Score separate held-out clips. Report misses by distance, false tracks on empty scenes, reacquisition time and the closest reliable detection. Verify which goal is which when Pupper starts facing either way. Fine-tune on representative footage if repeatable detection errors justify it. [Class labels](../follow_me_pupper/robot/coco.txt) · [Camera and detector](../follow_me_pupper/robot/follow_service.py)
2. **Give the harness a usable observation.** Preserve the camera’s capture time and clock through inference. Supply validity, confidence, ball bearing with units, and calibrated range or robot-relative position in metres (`x` forward, `y` left), goal color/bearing and path state (`clear`, `blocked`, `unknown`). Replay one good track and one loss. An expired observation must end pursuit with zero commanded travel. Treat an unknown path as a reason to stop and reobserve. Agree on maximum track age with the gait owner using measured speed and stopping distance.
3. **Demonstrate approach and avoidance.** Film approaches with the ball initially left and right. Mark the intended stopping position behind the ball relative to the attacking goal. Put a soft obstacle across the direct path. A passing run goes around it without contact, reacquires the ball and reaches the marked position. Remove the ball in another run and verify Pupper stops. Report attempts, successes, final position error and any false clearance. The Lab 5 follower provides target association and an obstacle heuristic; its person-specific stopping distance needs calibration for the ball. [Target tracking](../follow_me_pupper/lab/target_lock.py) · [Obstacle logic](../follow_me_pupper/lab/avoidance.py)

**Bring to the team review** the lab submission items and any gaps, labeled clips, a sample observation, trial results, and the clearest remaining failure. A teammate can roll balls and score footage while you run the detector. Coordinate one operator for camera, accelerator and motion access. Once these fundamentals work, the next defensive experiment is predicting where and when a ball crosses a taped goal line from a stationary goalie’s view. The gait teammate’s lateral reach measurements determine which shots are reachable. [Robot session procedure](../foundation/HARDWARE_CHECKLIST.md)
